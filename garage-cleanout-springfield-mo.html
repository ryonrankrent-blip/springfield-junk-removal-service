A garage cleanout is the bay, not the whole house

People call when they want to park in the garage again or when stored junk has reached the door. We sort the pile by your marks, load what goes, and haul it. Sorting here means separating what you pointed out from what stays. It does not mean we decide what is worth keeping. Call (417) 242-5370 with a plain description: one bay or two, how high the pile is, and whether a car is still inside.

If every room in the home is part of the job, use the house cleanout page and mention the garage so it is included. An estate where the family is still choosing what to keep is an estate cleanout. This page stays on the garage.

What usually goes

Cardboard, bins, shelves you are done with, junk lawn equipment, sports gear, and furniture that was stored out of the weather. A couch or dresser in the bay can go on this haul. If furniture is the only item and it is already by the door, furniture removal is enough.

What stays

The car, tools you still use, and any shelving you want left in place. Paint, solvents, fuel, and propane stay off the truck. Springfield's household chemical drop-off is listed on the pickup or drop-off page.

Heavy items and a loft

Tool chests, engines, tile, and stacks of pavers are weight, not just volume. A storage loft or a pull-down stair inside the garage is a second carry. Name both. A mattress or an appliance in the bay should be listed the same way.

How the price is built

There is no garage rate card and no price per bay. We quote three things. Volume is how full the space is, from a corner of boxes to a bay you cannot walk through. Weight is the difference between empty cardboard and a chest full of hardware. Access is the door, the driveway, stairs to a loft, and how far we carry items to a place the truck can legally stop. Those factors are written out on the junk removal cost page. A photo of the open door is the fastest way to keep the quote honest.

Same-day is often possible when the schedule is open. We do not promise it. Call with the day you need and we will tell you the first time we can be there.

Alleys, driveways, and HOA streets

Many older Springfield houses, especially near downtown and in neighborhoods with detached garages, load from an alley. Alley width, overhead lines, and whether another garage already blocks the lane change whether we can bring the truck to the door or have to carry to the street. Tell us which. A steep driveway does the same thing from the front.

Some homeowners associations limit truck parking, dumpster-style loads, or how long a vehicle can sit at the curb. We do not know your HOA's rule. Ask the association or the property manager before the day, then put the answer in the quote request. If the rule is "no trucks in the alley," we price the longer carry. We will not guess.

Say whether the overhead door opens. A door that does not move means we use the side door, and large furniture may not fit. A wall of shelves that has to come down is part of the job only if you want the shelves gone.

Garage, attic, basement, or a storage unit

The garage is one of three places a Springfield house stores what did not fit. Boxes above the ceiling are an attic cleanout. Junk under the house is a basement cleanout. A unit you rent off the property is a storage unit cleanout. You can book more than one on the same visit if you describe each space. A backyard building is shed removal, not a garage. Branches and bagged leaves are yard waste.

Other property types, including apartments and estates, are on the cleanouts page. If you would rather haul a few items yourself, the city landfill and recycling options are on the pickup or drop-off page. Those fees are the city's.
